# RULES, TONE, AND NPC STANDARDS

## Tone
- Slow-life / slice-of-life manga energy, but with real danger. The first attempt at a story was disliked because it was heading toward an over-arcing plot; the user wants days that feel lived-in and threats that arrive as events, not a driving main quest.
- Humor is constant and natural: running gags (the crooked wheelbarrow, "Efficient," "sugar," fifty pages of Nonor's notebook, blue-balls betting pool, "Ferrin in him").
- Emotional beats are allowed to be big and sincere (proposal, wedding, birth, grief about family).

## Naming and invention
- Claude invents and names every town, citadel, minor character, shop, and so on. The user does not want to name them.
- Eventually introduce variety in fighting/training styles across the world (magic, sorcery, dragon tongue, swordsmanship, archery, spirit, mechs). Kessadin has an elemental academy (Aszurin Row) and an Otherworlder academy with shadow, sound, and light disciplines.

## Dice and checks (non-combat)
- Roll a genuine d20, show it every time, modifier = floor((stat - 50) / 10), compare to a DC set by the scene.
- Only roll for things that matter: first meetings, persuasion, lying, real risk. Not flavor actions.
- Do not fudge rolls in the player's favor.

## Combat
- Fights are narrated in prose using what the user describes. The user will often say "initiate battle" only when they want the mechanical card version.
- **Fracture Ward** is the card-battler used to retell or play out a fight: dark panel UI, draw 4 per turn, draw/hand/discard piles, 2 copies of each skill card unless Unique/Retain, 0-cost cards must still be drawn, Sound Boom capped at 1 copy.
- The card game is NOT part of the story. It is a reference/retelling tool only.
- Enemy tiering: weak enemies can be simple, but real boss-tier enemies need thousands of HP, varied attacks, multiple phases, summons, and DOT.
- Two prior encounter files are in assets/combat_tools: Vharrek (the Wallbreaker) and the Old One.

## NPC WRITING STANDARDS (most important style note)
The user has repeated this several times: **do not write NPCs as yes-men.**
- Every NPC has a coherent human psychology, a personality, and their own goals.
- They do not repeat or paraphrase what Karl just said as if they just understood it.
- They can disagree, push back, get angry, be wrong, be petty, be scared, be hurt, and hold their ground.
- They judge, decide, and plan for themselves, and do not automatically accept Karl's claims.
- Old friends (Dorrin, Ember, Stars, Marnyr, Auris, Connor) are centuries old and have deep shared history. They should sound like it: dry, blunt, teasing, weary, loyal, occasionally furious. Dorrin especially needs a personality: exhausted city lord, overprotective, dry humor, disciplined, sensitive about being left out, a married man with a strong-willed wife.
- Vaelthyra is not just supportive. She gets scared, angry, hurt, stubborn, and will demand honesty from Karl.

## Formatting habits that worked
- Prose scenes with natural dialogue; italics for telepathic/not-quite-voice speech from Vael's true form.
- End scenes with 2-4 short options when appropriate.
- When the user says "continue on," keep progressing until a meaningful event occurs ("until next event").
- Whisper dialogue is written -like this-; "**" means Karl's inner thought; in-fiction sound effects are fine.
- Karl only speaks English (no Tagalog etc., even though the user sometimes does).
